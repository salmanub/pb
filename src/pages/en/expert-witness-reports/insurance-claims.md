## You Have Rights. We Have the Specialization to Defend Them.

After an incident, the insurance company appoints its own expert. This professional, while qualified, works for the company, not for you. Their goal is to assess the damage according to the company's standards. But is that assessment fair? Does it truly cover all the damages? What happens if the repair company they assign fails to perform?

A **party-appointed expert report** (or counter-appraisal) is your right as a policyholder. It is a technical report prepared by an expert you hire to have your own independent and objective valuation that defends your interests.

### Our Certified Specialization: Experts in Fire and Miscellaneous Risks (FMR)

To offer the highest guarantee in these types of cases, we hold a **Postgraduate Degree in Insurance Expertise in Fire and Miscellaneous Risks (known as IRD in Spain), obtained in 2007 through INESE**, the leading training center for the insurance sector in Spain.

This specialization means we don't just understand construction; we also **master the language, procedures, and fine print of insurance contracts**. We know what can be claimed, how each item should be valued, and how to argue technically against the company's expert.

### Key Scenarios Where a Certified Expert is Decisive

Our report is the tool you need to take control in situations such as:

#### **The Offered Settlement is Insufficient**
The insurer presents a damage assessment after a fire and gives you two options: let their company handle the repairs or pay you that amount. Thanks to our FMR specialization, we conduct a **detailed and independent damage assessment**, including items that are often overlooked, to renegotiate a higher settlement.

#### **Pressure to Sign a Release Form**
It is very common for the insurer to ask you to sign a "settlement receipt" or "release form" along with the offer. **Be careful!** Signing this document often means waiving future claims for that incident. Before you sign anything, our report allows you to ensure the amount offered is correct, giving you negotiating power.

#### **Breaches by the Repair Company**
You agree to the repair, the company assigned by the insurer receives an advance payment, and the work is unjustifiably delayed or poorly executed, as in this real case:
> *After a fire in April, by August the insurer's repair company had not yet begun the work despite having collected a 40% advance. We drafted an expert report that served as **irrefutable proof of the contractual breach.** This gave our client the legal basis to terminate the contract and take control of the remaining settlement.*
